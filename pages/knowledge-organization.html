Knowledge Organization

From cultural connections to a formal model

01 / Knowledge Modeling

Theoretical Model

The theoretical model was developed from the fourteen selected entities and the relationships identified during the initial idea conceptualisation. Each connection was expressed through natural-language statements, before introducing specific ontology terms or RDF properties.

The model highlights both the relation of each entity to James Bond and the connections among the selected items themselves. In this way, Bond's literary origins, institutional identity, audiovisual conventions, material associations, verbal markers and later cultural appearances can be read as parts of the same network of cultural identity.

The labels Origins, Character system, Material identity, Institutional identity, Verbal identity, Audiovisual identity and Cultural afterlife are analytical groupings introduced to organise the model and make the different dimensions of Bond's cultural identity easier to read. They are not intended as semantic classes or formal categories and are therefore not translated into the conceptual model or encoded in the ontology and RDF datasets.

02 / Semantic Formalisation

Conceptual Model

Starting from the entities and predicates expressed in natural language in the theoretical model, the conceptual model formalises them through RDF classes, properties and reusable URIs. Existing ontologies and controlled vocabularies were preferred whenever their semantics matched the intended entity or relation, while project-specific terms were introduced only where a suitable reusable term was not available.

The selection of reusable terms was based on their semantic definition and modelling role rather than on lexical similarity alone. External authority identifiers were retained to connect the selected entities with existing knowledge bases, while SKOS concepts were used for the project's classification layer.

Ontologies and vocabularies reused

The conceptual model combines terms from existing ontologies and vocabularies according to the semantic role required by each entity or relation. Reuse was preferred whenever an existing term adequately expressed the intended meaning; project-specific terms were introduced only where the available resources did not provide a sufficiently appropriate solution.

Core Semantic Web standards

RDF, RDFS, OWL and XML Schema datatypes provide the formal infrastructure of the model. RDF is used for typing statements, RDFS for resources, literals, subclass relations and schema-level definitions, OWL for the ontology itself and its classes and properties, and XSD for datatype values such as xsd:string and xsd:gYear.

Authority and classification sources

External identifiers connect the selected entities to existing authority records and Linked Open Data resources, including Wikidata, VIAF, SBN and the Library of Congress. The classification layer uses LCGFT concepts for Spy fiction, Spy films and Motion picture music, while First-person shooter is linked to Wikidata. These resources identify or classify project entities but are not treated as ontologies defining the 00LOD data model.

03 / Ontology Implementation

Bond Ontology

The conceptual model was implemented in Protégé as an OWL ontology. BondOntology formalises the project-specific classes and properties together with the semantic resources reused throughout the model.

Reused classes and properties are integrated directly into the ontology, while subclass and subproperty relations situate selected project terms within broader semantic structures. The SKOS-based classification layer used by the RDF datasets is also represented. The logical consistency of the ontology was checked with the HermiT reasoner.

Only a small set of project-specific terms was introduced:

Classes bond:Catchphrase bond:StockCharacter
Object properties bond:featuresNarrativeRole bond:hasRelatedCharacter bond:hasVisualMarker bond:hasMusicalMarker
Datatype property bond:hasCodeNumber

These terms were created only where the reused vocabularies did not provide a sufficiently precise representation of the concepts or relations required by the research question. Where possible, the local terms were connected to more general reused semantics through subclass or subproperty relations.

Ontology access

The ontology is available in Turtle serialization. Human-readable documentation will also be provided through LODE once the published ontology URI is publicly accessible.
